These walkthroughs take one person through the four things Avow has to get right. Start with the [agent starter](https://github.com/Avow-Finance/avow/tree/main/examples/starter), which shows limits holding in two minutes. Its sandbox registers your business and pays it test dollars, then your agent books within its authority, Avow refuses a booking past what's left, and the agent books again. The agent runs with only its own key, and the business's key stays sealed under your passphrase.

```bash
npx https://try.testnet.avow.finance/create-avow-agent.tgz my-agent
```

## The whole loop in one command

Once your agent holds a mandate, one command shows a kept booking and a late cancellation on Moderato in about ten minutes. Run it in the starter's folder, or in any folder whose agent you connected with `npx avow-sandbox connect`.

```bash
npx avow-sandbox loop
```

Your agent books one H100 for four minutes, twice, from Avow's demo GPU supplier, a test business, with a \$10 reserve each. Free cancellation ends two minutes after each booking, the window opens two minutes later and lasts four minutes, and the reserve releases at its end, eight minutes after booking.

1. **The kept booking.** The supplier accepts it, nothing is reported, and the lock gives the whole \$10 back to your business at its release date.
2. **The late cancellation.** Once free cancellation has ended, your agent asks Avow to record the cancellation, and Avow records it with its own time. The run waits 40 seconds past the end of free cancellation first, and 40 seconds after the cancellation before the report, since Avow confirms a late-cancellation charge only for a cancellation at least its clock margin from each of those moments and refers one any closer to a ruling. The margin is `clockMargin` in the testnet's `config.json`, 30 seconds, and the run reads it from there. When the time Avow recorded sits closer than the margin to either end of the late window, the run asks for no charge and says why. The demo supplier reports the late-cancellation charge the terms allow, half the reserve. Avow confirms it from its own record of the cancellation, and the lock sends the other \$5 back to your business at once. The charge pays the supplier ten minutes later, when the contest window closes, unless your business contests it first.

The run prints a link to check each booking, the report's transaction on the explorer and when the charge pays. Add `--reserve <dollars>` for another reserve, and the late cancellation always costs half of one.

It acts with only your agent's key, within the limits your administrator already signed, and approves nothing. Before it books, it checks that the mandate names the demo supplier and permits `cancel`, that two reserves fit in the authority left and in what the key can still move today, and it stops with the reason code when one doesn't. A run that stops after booking, for any reason, prints each reserve it left in your lock, that each comes back whole by its release date unless a charge the run asked for is confirmed, and the link to check it. A real supplier reports a late cancellation on its own. The demo supplier reports one only when the booking's own agent asks, signed with the key of the mandate the booking was made under, so the run asks it, and the charge still has to fit the terms and Avow's record of the cancellation.

The rest run the buyer and supplier quickstarts on one machine against Avow's hosted testnet service, and you play both sides. The buyer quickstart is a business and its agent, and the supplier quickstart is the business the agent books from. Every amount is test tUSD on Tempo's Moderato testnet, so nothing here moves real money.

| Walkthrough | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| [A kept booking](/docs/walkthroughs/kept-booking) | The supplier accepts, and the whole reserve goes back to the business at its release date |
| [A capped charge](/docs/walkthroughs/capped-charge) | The business cancels late, and the supplier is paid no more than the late-cancellation amount |
| [A disputed charge](/docs/walkthroughs/dispute) | The business contests a charge, the lock pauses it, Avow rules, and the lock pays as ruled |
| [Limits that hold](/docs/walkthroughs/limits) | A booking over the agent's limits is refused with a reason code, which the starter shows first |

## Set up both sides once

Build the quickstarts as the [welcome page](/welcome) shows. From the repository's root folder, make the three businesses. `AVOW_FACTORY` comes from the testnet's `config.json`, read here with `curl` and `jq`. Any factory in the SDK's `MODERATO_FACTORIES` counts, so a lock made from an earlier one still books.

```bash
export AVOW_FACTORY=$(curl -fsS https://try.testnet.avow.finance/config.json | jq -r .factory)
(cd examples/buyer && node dist/main.js init)
(cd examples/buyer && STATE_DIR=.avow-buyer-spare node dist/main.js init)
(cd packages/supplier && node dist/main.js init)
```

The second buyer is a spare, so a booking that has to be made again doesn't wait on a new registration. The supplier's `init` registers it with Avow's sandbox and prints its business id. Each buyer prints its account and lock. The buyer quickstart doesn't register through the sandbox yet, so send both buyers, with a name and country for each, to the person at Avow who invited you to the testnet. Nothing past this point works until Avow has registered them. Avow sends each buyer's account enough test tUSD for three \$500 reserves, and sends you each buyer's business id. The other settings are the testnet's own, published at https://try.testnet.avow.finance/config.json.

| Setting | Side | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `AVOW_FACTORY` | Both | Avow's lock factory, `factory` in `config.json` |
| `AVOW_API_URL` | Both | Avow's hosted API, `https://api.testnet.avow.finance` |
| `AVOW_TOKEN` | Both | The token reserves are held in |
| `BUYER_BUSINESS_ID` | Buyer | The business id Avow registered the buyer under, with a second one for the spare |
| `AVOW_ARBITER` | Buyer | The arbiter Avow's approvals name |
| `SUPPLIER_BUSINESS_ID` | Supplier | The business id the supplier's `init` printed |
| `AVOW_APPROVAL_KEYS` | Supplier | The address Avow signs authorizations with |

The quickstarts read their settings from the environment, so every terminal needs them. Put them in a file at the repository's root, here called `avow-settings.sh`, and keep it out of version control.

```bash
CONFIG=$(curl -fsS https://try.testnet.avow.finance/config.json)
export AVOW_FACTORY=$(echo "$CONFIG" | jq -r .factory)
export AVOW_API_URL=$(echo "$CONFIG" | jq -r .apiUrl)
export AVOW_TOKEN=$(echo "$CONFIG" | jq -r .token)
export AVOW_ARBITER=$(echo "$CONFIG" | jq -r .arbiter)
export AVOW_APPROVAL_KEYS=$(echo "$CONFIG" | jq -r .approvalKey)
export BUYER_BUSINESS_ID=<from Avow>
export SUPPLIER_BUSINESS_ID=<the businessId the supplier's init printed>
export SUPPLIER_URL=http://localhost:8790
```

The walkthroughs use two kinds of terminal. A supplier terminal works in `packages/supplier`, and a buyer terminal works in `examples/buyer`, each with the settings loaded. Open the first supplier terminal, start the supplier and leave it running. The buyer reaches it at `SUPPLIER_URL`.

```bash
cd packages/supplier
source ../../avow-settings.sh
node dist/main.js serve
```

Then open a buyer terminal and authorize the agent. Keep the default limits, which let the agent hold three \$500 bookings at once.

```bash
cd examples/buyer
source ../../avow-settings.sh
node dist/main.js enroll
```

Every later command says which kind of terminal it runs in. A second terminal of either kind opens the same way.

## The order that fits an hour

A booking with the default window takes 42 minutes from `book` to its release, and a dispute takes about as long, so the walkthroughs run side by side. Times are from the first `book`.

| Time | Buyer | Supplier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Books the dispute's booking, then the capped charge's | Accepts each booking on its own |
| 0:02 | Books the kept booking in a second terminal, and leaves it waiting for the release | Accepts it |
| 0:04 | Tries the two bookings in [Limits that hold](/docs/walkthroughs/limits), which are refused | Nothing |
| 0:08 | Cancels the dispute's booking and the capped charge's booking, now that free cancellation has ended | Nothing |
| 0:09 | Nothing | Reports a late cancellation on both, and Avow confirms each within five minutes |
| About 0:13 | Contests the dispute's charge as soon as Avow confirms it, since the contest window lasts ten minutes | Nothing |
| About 0:25 | Nothing | The capped charge pays \$250 once its contest window has closed |
| 0:44 | The kept booking's reserve comes back | Nothing |
| By 0:50 | Avow rules on the dispute within 30 minutes of the pause, by about 0:43, and the lock pays five minutes after the ruling | The ruling's share arrives |

Name each booking by the commitment id `book` printed, since `cancel` and `contest` otherwise take the latest booking. The agent's key can move \$1,500 a day at the default limits, which is exactly these three reserves. If a booking has to be made again the same day, switch to the spare in a buyer terminal with `export STATE_DIR=.avow-buyer-spare BUYER_BUSINESS_ID=<the spare's id>`, run `enroll` once, and book from there.

## Testnet timing

Avow's testnet locks run on minutes where a live lock would run on days, so a whole dispute fits in an hour.

| Step | On testnet |
| --- | --- |
| Free cancellation ends | 6 minutes after `book`, with the default window |
| A no-show can be reported | 14 minutes after `book` |
| The reserve is released, and the last charge can be reported | 42 minutes after `book` |
| Avow confirms or refers a report | Within 5 minutes of the report |
| The business can contest a confirmed charge | For 10 minutes after Avow confirms it |
| Avow rules on a paused charge | Within 30 minutes of the pause |
| The lock pays a ruling | 5 minutes after Avow posts it |
| The arbiter can rule | Until 90 minutes after the pause, when the lock splits a charge nobody ruled on |

Every transaction is on the [Moderato explorer](https://explore.testnet.tempo.xyz). Search for your lock to follow each booking's reserve, report, contest and payment.
