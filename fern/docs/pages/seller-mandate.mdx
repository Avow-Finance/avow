A seller's mandate is the authority a business's administrator signs for one selling agent. A buyer's mandate limits what an agent may reserve. A seller's mandate limits what an agent may promise, which is what it sells, at what price, how much, how far ahead, on what charges and to whom.

`@avow-finance/spec` holds the message, its signed form and the check. Avow's sandbox doesn't enforce a seller's mandate yet, so today a selling agent's signing key is still its only limit, and this page says so until that changes. Nothing here should be read as a limit a selling agent is held to today.

## The message

A `SellerMandate` is EIP-712 typed data for the business account's root key to sign, under the business's own lock. It binds one signing key, the key the business registered for its agent to quote and accept with, and hands no authority on to another agent. Its `replaces` field names the hash of the mandate it takes the place of, or null.

The spec holds the message and nothing around it yet. Nothing stores a mandate, revokes one or follows one that replaces another, and the check takes whether a mandate was revoked from whoever calls it.

Version 1 covers sales of `service/1` and `job/1`. Terms under any other template are refused with `seller_mandate.template_not_covered`.

## What it holds

| Field | What it limits |
| --- | --- |
| `limits` | What the agent may sell. Each line names a category, `service` or `compute-job`, and an item, with the lowest price for a unit and the most units in one order and in one UTC day. A line whose item is null covers every other item in its category |
| `maxOrderValue` | The most one order may come to |
| `maxOutstanding` | The most the agent may have promised and not yet delivered, as the agreed price of every sale from agreement to delivery |
| `maxDeliveryHorizon` | The longest from agreement to the delivery deadline |
| `minLateCancellationBps`, `minNoShowBps` | The lowest charges the agent may offer, as shares of the price |
| `maxFreeCancelWindow` | The longest a buyer of a service may cancel at no charge |
| `buyers` | The only businesses the agent may sell to, when any is named |
| `excludedBuyers` | Businesses the agent may never sell to, named only when `buyers` is empty |
| `excludedCountries` | Countries the agent may not sell into |
| `requiresBusinessCheck` | Whether a buyer has to have passed Avow's business check |
| `negotiable` | The terms the agent may move when it bargains. Version 1 takes none, so every sale is on its offer's own terms |
| `approvalRule` | Whether a person decides a sale: never, always, or above an order value under `maxOrderValue` |
| `expiry` | When the agent's authority to sell ends |

Amounts are in micros of the mandate's currency, and times are in seconds.

### Lines, and why an item's line can only loosen

An item is a service's name or a job's kind of work, matched by its exact name. A job counts as one unit whose price is the job's price.

An item's own line governs it. An item with no line of its own falls to its category's line for every other item, and an item with neither can't be sold.

An agent names its own items. If an item's line were tighter than the line for every other item, the agent could sell the same thing under another name and get the looser limits. So `sellerMandateProblem` refuses a mandate where an item's line has a higher floor or fewer units than its category's line for every other item. The line for every other item is the baseline, and an item's line is an exception that allows more.

Every item governed by the line for every other item shares that line's units for the day. Adding an item never adds to what the administrator signed for.

A floor of zero puts no floor on the price. The templates still require a price above zero, so no sale is free.

### One way to write a mandate

A mandate that passes `sellerMandateProblem` hashes in one way only. Lines come in order by category, then the line for every other item, then by item. Each list of businesses and countries is in ascending order with nothing repeated. The mandate holds exactly the fields the signed form names, text is well-formed Unicode, addresses and hashes are in lower case, and every amount fits the signed form. A mandate whose limits conflict is refused too, such as one order's most above what may be outstanding, or a floor above one order's most.

## Asking for one

A selling agent asks for a seller's mandate through an [approval request](/docs/reference/approval-requests) of the `seller_mandate` kind. It sends the terms it wants, which are every field above. The fields that say which mandate this is, whose and for which key stay out of the ask, and those are `mandateId`, `businessId`, `account`, `signingKey`, `nonce` and `replaces`. `sellerMandateTermsOf` reads a mandate's terms, and `carriesSellerMandateTerms` says whether a mandate carries exactly the terms an agent asked for.

An agent can ask and can't sign. Only the business account's root key signs a seller's mandate, and it signs one that carries the approved terms with nothing moved.

A mandate's `mandateId` is 32 random bytes. A sale's record is to show a buyer the mandate's hash, and the random bytes keep anyone from finding the limits behind that hash by guessing them.

## The check

`checkSellerMandate` is pure and never throws. A seller's agent is to run it before it signs an agreement, and Avow is to run the same code on the same inputs when a seller agrees to terms and again before it approves the buyer's reserve. It takes the mandate, the key that signed, the sale's terms, the offer a service's terms book, what Avow's records say of the buyer, and what the capacity ledger already holds.

It refuses in this order.

| Reason | When |
| --- | --- |
| `message.invalid` | What the check was told is malformed, such as a clock or a signing key that isn't one |
| `seller_mandate.invalid` | The mandate isn't well formed |
| `seller_mandate.expired`, `seller_mandate.revoked` | The mandate has ended, or the caller says the administrator revoked it |
| `seller_mandate.key_mismatch` | Another key signed the sale |
| `message.invalid` | The buyer, the ledger or the terms profile is malformed |
| `counterparty.not_eligible` | The buyer fails the counterparty rules |
| A `terms.*` or `dependency.*` reason | The terms aren't sound on their own, by the rules a buyer's mandate check holds them to |
| `seller_mandate.template_not_covered` | The terms are neither a service's nor a job's |
| `seller_mandate.currency_not_allowed` | The terms are in another currency |
| `seller_mandate.item_not_allowed` | No line covers the item |
| `message.invalid` | A service comes with no offer, an offer its template refuses or another business's offer, or a job comes with one |
| `order.terms_changed` | A service's terms aren't exactly the ones its offer builds |
| `message.invalid`, `message.expired` | A service's terms were built from a moment more than 60 seconds ahead, or their supplier can no longer accept |
| `seller_mandate.under_price_floor` | The price for each unit is under the line's floor |
| `seller_mandate.over_delivery_horizon` | Delivery is due later than the horizon allows |
| `seller_mandate.charges_not_allowed` | A charge is under its floor, a service's free cancellation lasts too long, or a job's no-show can't be reported by its delivery deadline |
| `seller_mandate.over_order_value` | The order comes to more than one order may |
| `seller_mandate.over_units_per_order` | The order takes more units than the line allows |
| `seller_mandate.over_daily_units` | The order would take the day's units past the line's limit |
| `seller_mandate.over_outstanding` | The order would take what's promised and undelivered past the limit |

A sale inside every limit and above the approval rule's amount comes back as `needs_human` with `approval.human_required`. The caller holds it until the administrator approves that one sale. Nothing lets a person approve one sale yet.

The arbiter and the day-90 share in a sale's terms are Avow's to hold to its own when it approves the reserve, as for any commitment.

### A service is sold on its offer's terms

A service's terms have to be exactly the ones `serviceOfferTerms` builds from the offer version they book. So the price for each unit is the offer's whatever the quantity, and no discount gets under the floor. The offer has to be the mandate's own business's. A job books no offer, and its floor is on the job's whole price. A job is read from its version 3 terms, which name the compute it runs on.

### Time

A limit on a length of time is held exactly wherever one clock measures both of its ends.

- A service's lead time and free-cancellation window are lengths its offer states. They meet the mandate's horizon and longest free cancellation with nothing to spare.
- A service's terms are built from a moment the buyer's order names, on the buyer's clock. That moment may be up to 60 seconds ahead of the clock of whoever runs the check, and no more. So a service's delivery falls at most the horizon plus 60 seconds after the check.
- A job's horizon runs from the clock of whoever runs the check to the job's delivery deadline, and is held exactly.
- A job's free cancellation ends when work starts, and whoever agrees the job agrees when that is. The mandate's longest free cancellation is for a service and doesn't read a job, so nothing bounds how long a job's buyer can cancel at no charge.

### Counterparties

The buyer is checked before anything about the sale, so a business the mandate won't deal with hears that and learns nothing else.

The check reads three facts about the buyer from Avow's records, its business id, its country and whether it passed Avow's business check. On testnet every business check is simulated and a country is the one the business declared. Any surface that shows these rules labels them that way, as "Passed Avow's simulated business check" and a self-declared country. Neither makes a business verified.

A mandate that excludes any country refuses a buyer whose country isn't known.

### Capacity

The day's units and what's outstanding come from a ledger the caller keeps, counted apart from the sale being checked. `saleFacts` reads a sale's category, item, units and price from its terms, and `sellingLimitFor` gives the line that governs it, so a ledger counts by the same line the check uses. Counting and recording have to happen in one step wherever sales can arrive at once, and that step belongs to whoever keeps the ledger.

## Listing an offer

`sellerOfferProblem` reads an offer version before anyone orders. The offer has to be for an item the mandate covers, within the limits the check holds an offer to, and its largest booking has to be within the units and the value one order may come to. A price so small that one unit's charges round to nothing builds no terms, and isn't listed. So every booking a listed offer invites is one the check would pass on those counts, under a profile its terms are sound in. An offer binds nobody, and this refuses early what the check would refuse at the sale.

## When Avow approves a reserve for a sale

`saleApprovalProblem` holds one rule for Avow's reserve approval. Avow is to approve a buyer's reserve for a sale only once the supplier's acceptance deadline is 15 minutes away or less by Avow's clock, exactly. Before that it answers `approval.acceptance_too_far`, and the buyer asks again closer to the deadline. A job can still run for days after its supplier accepts.

The rule bounds how long a reserve can still be accepted after its seller's mandate ends or is revoked.

## What a buyer hears

The amounts a seller's mandate sets stay with the seller. `buyerFacingReason` passes on two of the check's answers, that the sale is approved and that the seller won't deal with this buyer. Every other answer becomes `supplier.not_authorized`, which says the supplier's agent isn't authorized to sell on these terms and names no limit. The seller's agent hears the specific reason.

A buyer told that a person has to approve would wait for nothing today, so it hears `supplier.not_authorized` until one sale can be approved.

A buyer that varies its orders can still infer roughly where a limit sits from which of them are refused.

## Two layers, and no chain

A buyer's mandate has three enforcing layers. A seller's mandate is to have two, the seller's own agent and Avow. The lock belongs to the buyer and never reads a seller's mandate, and the signing key moves no money, so Tempo's keychain has nothing to hold. Once a buyer's reserve is on the lock, the lock takes an acceptance signed by the seller's key until the acceptance deadline, whatever the seller's mandate says by then.
