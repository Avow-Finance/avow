A seller's mandate is the authority a business's administrator signs for one selling agent. A buyer's mandate limits what an agent may reserve. A seller's mandate limits what an agent may promise, which is what it sells, at what price, how much, how far ahead, on what charges and to whom.

`@avow-finance/spec` holds the message, its signed form and the check. Avow doesn't enforce a seller's mandate yet, and it doesn't serve one either. The SDK's selling agent reads its own mandates and holds itself to the newest, as [The agent holds itself to it](#the-agent-holds-itself-to-it) sets out, so a selling agent with no mandate from Avow sells as it did before, with its signing key as its only limit.

## The message

A `SellerMandate` is EIP-712 typed data for the business account's root key to sign, under the business's own lock. It binds one signing key, the key the business registered for its agent to quote and accept with, and hands no authority on to another agent. Its `replaces` field names the hash of the mandate it takes the place of, or null.

The spec holds the messages and the checks. Where a deployment turns seller's mandates on, Avow's sandbox stores each one the business account signed, sealed under a key only Avow's API holds, so a mandate's terms never sit in its database in the clear, and it opens one only to answer the business and its selling agent. Avow's testnet has them off until it turns them on. Nothing stores a revocation yet, so the check takes whether a mandate was revoked from whoever calls it. [Replacing and revoking](#replacing-and-revoking) says how a caller works that out.

Version 1 covers sales of `service/1` and `job/1`. Terms under any other template are refused with `seller_mandate.template_not_covered`. That includes GPU reservations under `gpu-reservation/1`, which no seller's mandate covers yet, so a supplier sells them under the rules it always has and an agreement for one names no mandate.

## What it holds

| Field | What it limits |
| --- | --- |
| `signingKey` | The one key that may sell under the mandate |
| `currency` | The currency every amount is in, and the only one the agent may sell in |
| `limits` | What the agent may sell. Each line names a category, `service` or `compute-job`, and an item, with the lowest price for a unit and the most units in one order and in one UTC day. A line whose item is null covers every other item in its category |
| `maxOrderValue` | The most one order may come to |
| `maxOutstanding` | The most the agent may have promised and not yet delivered, as the agreed price of every sale from agreement to delivery |
| `maxDeliveryHorizon` | The longest from agreement to the delivery deadline |
| `minLateCancellationBps`, `minNoShowBps` | The lowest charges the agent may offer, as shares of the price |
| `maxFreeCancelWindow` | The longest a buyer of a service may cancel at no charge |
| `buyers` | The only businesses the agent may sell to, when any is named |
| `excludedBuyers` | Businesses the agent may never sell to, named only when `buyers` is empty |
| `excludedCountries` | Countries the agent may not sell into |
| `requiresBusinessCheck` | Whether a buyer has to have passed Avow's business check |
| `negotiable` | The terms the agent may move when it bargains. Version 1 takes none, so every sale is on its offer's own terms |
| `approvalRule` | Whether a person decides a sale: never, always, or above an order value under `maxOrderValue` |
| `expiry` | When the agent's authority to sell ends, by the end of the year 9999 at the latest |

Amounts are in micros of the mandate's currency, and times are in seconds.

### Lines, and why an item's line can only loosen

An item is a service's name or a job's kind of work, matched by its exact name. A job counts as one unit whose price is the job's price.

An item's own line governs it. An item with no line of its own falls to its category's line for every other item, and an item with neither can't be sold.

An agent names its own items. If an item's line were tighter than the line for every other item, the agent could sell the same thing under another name and get the looser limits. So `sellerMandateProblem` refuses a mandate where an item's line has a higher floor or fewer units than its category's line for every other item. The line for every other item is the baseline, and an item's line is an exception that allows more.

Every item governed by the line for every other item shares that line's units for the day. Adding an item never adds to what the administrator signed for.

A floor of zero puts no floor on the price. The templates still require a price above zero, so no sale is free.

### One way to write a mandate

A mandate that passes `sellerMandateProblem` hashes in one way only. Lines come in order by category, then the line for every other item, then by item. Each list of businesses and countries is in ascending order with nothing repeated, and a business is named by its id. An item's name reads on one line, with no control character, no mark that turns text around or hides it, no double quote and no space at either end, since an agent writes it and a person reads it in quotes on the card they sign. The mandate holds exactly the fields the signed form names, text is well-formed Unicode, addresses and hashes are in lower case, and every amount fits the signed form. A mandate whose limits conflict is refused too, such as one order's most above what may be outstanding, or a floor above one order's most.

## Asking for one

A selling agent is to ask for a seller's mandate through an [approval request](/docs/reference/approval-requests) of the `seller_mandate` kind, which Avow doesn't take yet. The ask holds the terms the agent wants, which are every field above. The fields that say which mandate this is and whose stay out of it, and those are `mandateId`, `businessId`, `account`, `nonce` and `replaces`. `sellerMandateTermsOf` reads a mandate's terms, and `carriesSellerMandateTerms` says whether a well-formed mandate carries exactly the terms an agent asked for.

An agent can ask and can't sign. Only the business account's root key signs a seller's mandate, and it signs one that carries the approved terms with nothing moved.

A selling agent asks from code with `askToSell(terms)` on `sandboxServices`, from a terminal with `npx avow-sandbox ask-to-sell --terms <file>`, or with the MCP server's `request_seller_mandate` tool. Each takes the terms whole, as the spec writes them, signs the ask with the agent's enrolled key and gives the reason itself: `new_seller` when no mandate is in force for the signing key, and `seller_limits` when one is. The terms are the person's to set, so an agent asks its person for every limit and never chooses them itself. The ask answers with a link the administrator opens to decide with their passkey, and nothing an agent can reach approves it. `awaitSellRequest` and `ask-to-sell` read where the request stands every few seconds until it settles, as connecting does, and `request_status` reads it once.

Avow doesn't take seller's mandate requests yet and refuses them with `approval_request.kind_unavailable`. Every one of the three then says plainly that Avow doesn't take them yet and that nothing was asked, and the agent sells as it did before.

A mandate's `mandateId` is 32 random bytes. A sale's record is to show a buyer the mandate's hash, and the random bytes keep anyone from finding the limits behind that hash by guessing them.

## The agreement names it

A `TermsAgreement` is what both sides sign to agree a sale. It can name the seller's mandate the supplier's agent agreed under, in `sellerMandate`, so the record of a sale says whose authority it was made on. The hash is the one `sellerMandateHash` gives, which is under the selling business's own lock, while the agreement itself signs under the buyer's. An agreement that names one signs at version 2, with the hash as its last field. One that names none signs at version 1, exactly as before, so every sale made under no seller's mandate reads as it always has. `termsAgreementVersion` gives the version, and `parseSignedAgreement` refuses an envelope whose version isn't the one its message signs at.

Naming a mandate is a statement both sides signed, and no proof that anyone checked the mandate. Avow's API doesn't take an agreement that names a seller's mandate yet, so a buyer can't fund a reserve on one. The sale checks being built for Avow lift that, once Avow can check the mandate an agreement names. Where Avow stores a seller's mandate, it serves it only to its business and its selling agent, so no buyer can look one up. Whoever writes an agreement decides what it names, so a seller's agent signs one that names a mandate only when the mandate is its own. In a service order or a job the seller's agent writes the agreement, and `sandboxServices`, the mailbox and the job demo name the newest mandate in force for its signing key, the one they held the sale to, and name none when no mandate is in force. The buyer adds nothing to it, and checks the seller's signature at the version the agreement signs at. The supplier adapter in `@avow-finance/supplier` sells only GPU reservations, which no mandate covers, and is sent its agreements by the buyer, so it refuses one that names any. A buyer that sees a hash here learns which mandate the agreement names, and nothing about what that mandate allows.

## The check

`checkSellerMandate` is pure and never throws. A seller's agent is to run it before it signs an agreement, and Avow is to run the same code on the same inputs when a seller agrees to terms and again before it approves the buyer's reserve. It takes the mandate, the key that signed, the sale's terms, the offer a service's terms book, what Avow's records say of the buyer, what the capacity ledger already holds, and the one sale a person approved, if any.

It refuses in this order.

| Reason | When |
| --- | --- |
| `message.invalid` | What the check was told is malformed, such as a clock or a signing key that isn't one |
| `seller_mandate.invalid` | The mandate isn't well formed |
| `seller_mandate.expired`, `seller_mandate.revoked` | The mandate has ended, or the caller says the administrator revoked it |
| `seller_mandate.key_mismatch` | Another key signed the sale |
| `message.invalid` | The buyer, the ledger or the terms profile is malformed, or the approved sale handed in is no well-formed sale |
| `counterparty.not_eligible` | The buyer fails the counterparty rules |
| A `terms.*` or `dependency.*` reason | The terms aren't sound on their own, by the rules a buyer's mandate check holds them to |
| `seller_mandate.template_not_covered` | The terms are neither a service's nor a job's |
| `seller_mandate.currency_not_allowed` | The terms are in another currency |
| `seller_mandate.item_not_allowed` | No line covers the item |
| `message.invalid` | A service comes with no offer, an offer its template refuses or another business's offer, or a job comes with one |
| `order.terms_changed` | A service's terms aren't exactly the ones its offer builds |
| `message.invalid`, `message.expired` | A service's terms were built from a moment more than 60 seconds ahead, or their supplier can no longer accept |
| `seller_mandate.under_price_floor` | The price for each unit is under the line's floor |
| `seller_mandate.over_delivery_horizon` | Delivery is due later than the horizon allows |
| `seller_mandate.charges_not_allowed` | A charge is under its floor, a service's free cancellation lasts too long, or a job's no-show can't be reported by its delivery deadline |
| `seller_mandate.over_order_value` | The order comes to more than one order may |
| `seller_mandate.over_units_per_order` | The order takes more units than the line allows |
| `seller_mandate.over_daily_units` | The order would take the day's units past the line's limit |
| `seller_mandate.over_outstanding` | The order would take what's promised and undelivered past the limit |

A sale inside every limit and above the approval rule's amount comes back as `needs_human` with `approval.human_required`, unless it is exactly the sale the check was told a person approved. The spec holds a [request for that approval](/docs/reference/approval-requests#asking-a-person-to-approve-one-sale), which names the signing key, the buyer, the whole offer version and the units. Whoever records sales keeps an approval to one sale and to a day, since the check reads only what it's handed. Avow doesn't take that request yet, so nothing lets a person approve one sale today. An approval lifts no limit, since every limit is checked first, and no job is approved this way.

The arbiter and the day-90 share in a sale's terms are Avow's to hold to its own when it approves the reserve, as for any commitment.

### A service is sold on its offer's terms

A service's terms have to be exactly the ones `serviceOfferTerms` builds from the offer version they book. So the price for each unit is the offer's whatever the quantity, and no discount gets under the floor. The offer has to be the mandate's own business's. A job books no offer, and its floor is on the job's whole price. A job is read from its version 3 terms, which name the compute it runs on.

### Time

A limit on a length of time is held exactly wherever one clock measures both of its ends.

- A service's lead time and free-cancellation window are lengths its offer states. They meet the mandate's horizon and longest free cancellation with nothing to spare.
- A service's terms are built from a moment the buyer's order names, on the buyer's clock. That moment may be up to 60 seconds ahead of the clock of whoever runs the check, and no more. So a service's delivery falls at most the horizon plus 60 seconds after the check.
- A job's horizon runs from the clock of whoever runs the check to the job's delivery deadline, and is held exactly.
- A job's free cancellation ends when work starts, and whoever agrees the job agrees when that is. The mandate's longest free cancellation is for a service and doesn't read a job, so nothing bounds how long a job's buyer can cancel at no charge.

### Counterparties

The buyer is checked before anything about the sale, so a business the mandate won't deal with hears that and learns nothing else.

The check reads three facts about the buyer from Avow's records, its business id, its country and whether it passed Avow's business check. On testnet every business check is simulated and a country is the one the business declared. Any surface that shows these rules labels them that way, as "Passed Avow's simulated business check" and a self-declared country. Neither makes a business verified.

A mandate that excludes any country refuses a buyer whose country isn't known.

### Capacity

The day's units and what's outstanding come from a ledger the caller keeps, counted apart from the sale being checked. `saleFacts` reads a sale's category, item, units and price from its terms, and `sellingLimitFor` gives the line that governs it, so a ledger counts by the same line the check uses. Counting and recording have to happen in one step wherever sales can arrive at once, and that step belongs to whoever keeps the ledger.

## Listing an offer

`sellerOfferProblem` reads an offer version before anyone orders. The offer has to be for an item the mandate covers, within the limits the check holds an offer to, and its largest booking has to be within the units and the value one order may come to. A price so small that one unit's charges round to nothing builds no terms, and isn't listed. So every booking a listed offer invites is one the check would pass on those counts, under a profile its terms are sound in. An offer binds nobody, and this refuses early what the check would refuse at the sale.

## When Avow approves a reserve for a sale

`saleApprovalProblem` holds one rule for Avow's reserve approval. Avow is to approve a buyer's reserve for a sale only once the supplier's acceptance deadline is 15 minutes away or less by Avow's clock, exactly. Before that it answers `approval.acceptance_too_far`, and the buyer asks again closer to the deadline. A job can still run for days after its supplier accepts.

The rule bounds how long a reserve can still be accepted after its seller's mandate ends or is revoked.

## Replacing and revoking

An administrator changes a selling agent's limits by signing a new mandate for the same signing key, whose `replaces` names the hash of the one before it. The mandates a key has held travel together, newest first, with the revocation of the key or null, and `parseSellerMandates` reads them.

`sellerMandateChainProblem` reads them as one chain, and it's the only way to one. Every mandate is well formed and names the business, the account and the signing key the caller expects. Each names the one before it, and the oldest names none. The check works out each hash itself, so a mandate can't be left out of the middle or slipped in. The business account has to have signed every one of them, the replaced ones too. A buyer's mandates end at the one its key carries on Tempo's keychain. A seller's have no such anchor, so the caller says whose mandates it expects, and ones another account signed for the same key are refused.

| Reason | When |
| --- | --- |
| `message.schema_unknown`, `message.wrong_chain`, `message.wrong_contract` | A mandate or the revocation sits in another envelope |
| `seller_mandate.invalid` | A mandate isn't well formed |
| `seller_mandate.key_mismatch` | A mandate binds another signing key |
| `seller_mandate.chain_mismatch` | A mandate names another business or account, or doesn't name the one before it |
| `seller_mandate.revoked` | A revocation of the key came with the mandates |
| `seller_mandate.signature_invalid` | The account didn't sign one of the mandates |
| `message.invalid` | Anything else is malformed, such as a revocation for another agent |

A `SellerKeyRevocation` is the administrator's word that a selling agent's authority has ended, for good. The business account's root key signs it under the business's own lock. It names the business, the account and the agent's signing key, and no mandate. It carries no time and never expires.

A revocation ends every seller's mandate for that business, account and key under that lock, whenever each was signed. A signature carries no time, so nothing could say whether a mandate came before a revocation or after it, and a rule that needs no order is the only one a reader can hold. So a revoked key is to be given no seller's mandate again, and its agent sells again under a new key. A buyer's revoked agent key has Tempo's keychain to refuse it. A seller's has no chain behind it, so whoever stores mandates has to refuse.

`sellerMandateChainProblem` answers `seller_mandate.revoked` when a revocation of the key comes with the mandates, without reading the revocation's signature. That answer means a revocation was served. A forged one can only stop an agent selling, which whoever serves the mandates can do by serving none. Whoever records a revocation checks it first, with `sellerKeyRevocationSignatureProblem`, and `revokesSellerKey` says whether one names a given agent or mandate.

Revoking is to stop every new promise and leave every commitment already made in force. It takes no key off a lock, so a reserve Avow approved before the revocation can still be accepted until its acceptance deadline, which the 15-minute rule above is there to bound.

The check reads only what it's handed, so four things fall to whoever stores mandates, which is Avow on a deployment that stores them.

- Avow stores mandates sealed only where a deployment turns them on, and nothing stores a revocation yet. No SDK call or tool builds or signs a mandate or a revocation. Only the account's root key signs one.
- A reader can't see a newer mandate or a revocation its server left out. Avow is to cover that, by approving a reserve only for a sale under the newest mandate of a key nobody revoked, and by serving a revoked key's revocation with every chain for it.
- A reader can't see a second mandate signed to replace the same one, or a second first mandate for a key. Whoever stores mandates is to refuse both, so a key has one chain.
- A revocation holds under the lock it was signed for. A business that moves to a new lock is to carry its revocations over before it takes a mandate there.

## What a buyer hears

The amounts a seller's mandate sets stay with the seller. `buyerFacingReason` passes on two of the check's answers, that the sale is approved and that the seller won't deal with this buyer. Every other answer becomes `supplier.not_authorized`, which says the supplier's agent isn't authorized to sell on these terms and names no limit. The seller's agent hears the specific reason.

A buyer told that a person has to approve would wait for nothing today, so it hears `supplier.not_authorized` until one sale can be approved.

A buyer that varies its orders can still infer roughly where a limit sits from which of them are refused.

## The agent holds itself to it

The SDK's selling agent is the first of the two layers. Before `publish` lists an offer, and before `sell` quotes an order or accepts one, it reads every seller's mandate its signing key has held from Avow at `SANDBOX_PATHS.sellerMandates`, signed by that key under its business's lock. It reads them with `parseSellerMandates` and `sellerMandateChainProblem`, and acts under the newest.

- **No mandate.** Avow doesn't serve the route yet, and a key it holds no mandate for is to get a 404 too. Either way no seller's mandate is in force, and the agent sells as it did before. `sellerStanding()` and every selling answer say so in plain words, and say that Avow doesn't enforce seller limits yet.
- **An offer.** `publish` checks the offer with `sellerOfferProblem` and refuses one the mandate doesn't allow with a `SellerMandateRefusal` carrying the spec's reason, such as `seller_mandate.under_price_floor`. Nothing is kept.
- **A sale.** `sell` checks the terms it would sign with `checkSellerMandate` before the quote goes out, and again before it accepts, so a mandate replaced, ended or revoked in between holds. The agreement in a quote it sends names the mandate it checked the sale against, and signs at version 2. A refusal frees the capacity the order took, sends nothing and leaves the order to lapse.
- **A chain that doesn't read.** Mandates the business account didn't sign, a revoked key or an answer that isn't a chain stop the agent selling, with the reason.

- **A job.** The mailbox's `sell` and the job demo hold a job the same way, on the exact version 3 terms the quote signs, once the compute it relies on is booked, and again before they accept. The quote names the mandate, and a refusal sends nothing. The compute booking stays, as it does when a buyer refuses a quote.
- **The mandate the agreement names.** Before it accepts a service or a job, the agent checks the mandate in force is the one the quote's agreement names. When an administrator replaced it, or it ended, or the agreement names none and one is in force now, the agent doesn't accept, and the order lapses. So an agreement never names a mandate the sale wasn't held to.

The agent counts its own ledger from the quotes it keeps in its `.avow` folder. A sale it quoted today under the same line of limits counts toward the day's units while it can still bind the seller or binds it, and its price counts as outstanding until it's due. Another agent with the same key, in another folder, isn't counted, so run one seller on a key. Services and jobs count in one ledger, since a mandate's limits hold across everything the seller sells. The services file keeps the services it quoted and `job-sales` the jobs, and every check reads both in the same turn as the sale it records.

Avow's records of a buyer aren't open to a selling agent yet, so the agent reads a buyer's country as unknown and treats it as not having passed a business check. A mandate that excludes any country, or requires a check, refuses every sale until they are. Avow takes no request to approve one sale yet, so a sale the mandate hands to a person is refused as `approval.human_required`, and its buyer sees the order lapse.

The agent holds itself to its mandate in its own process, and a seller running other code isn't held by this. Avow is to be the second layer, and it enforces nothing for a seller today.

## Two layers, and no chain

A buyer's mandate has three enforcing layers. A seller's mandate is to have two, the seller's own agent and Avow. The lock belongs to the buyer and never reads a seller's mandate, and the signing key moves no money, so Tempo's keychain has nothing to hold. Once a buyer's reserve is on the lock, the lock takes an acceptance signed by the seller's key until the acceptance deadline, whatever the seller's mandate says by then.
