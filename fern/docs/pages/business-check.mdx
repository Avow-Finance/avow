A sandbox business can ask Avow to check its company registration and the people behind it. The business's own code starts the check and reads where it stands, both signed by the account's root key, and a person finishes it on the checker's page. The business needs no console session, though the checker sends that person to Avow's console when they finish.

On testnet the checker, Didit, runs in its own sandbox. Every check there is simulated, and each answer that names a checker says so with `simulated: true`. A pass on testnet confirms nothing about a real business.

## What a check changes

A check changes no standing of the business and no limit on its agents. The business stays a sandbox business, its commitments stay test data, and its agents' mandates hold exactly what they held before.

One thing follows from a pass, for a business that has also registered as a sandbox supplier. Avow lists its account in the testnet's supplier registry. A buyer's lock that its business opened to listed suppliers then accepts a reserve for that account without the buyer naming it first.

The registry holds a hash of the check's record and the time of the check, and nothing else about it. The record names the checker and says the check was simulated, and the listing carries no such flag. So a listing on testnet says a simulated check passed, and nothing about a real business.

## When a lock turns a supplier away

A lock takes a reserve for a supplier its business named, or for a listed supplier while the business keeps the lock open to those. Avow checks this before it approves a reserve, so an agent hears the reason before it signs a transaction the lock would refuse. The lock decides again when the reserve lands.

| Code | The lock's reason | What changes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `supplier.not_named` | The business never named the supplier, and the lock isn't open to listed suppliers | The business names the supplier, or opens the lock with its root key |
| `supplier.removed` | The business took the supplier off its lock, which a listing never undoes | The business names the supplier again |
| `supplier.not_listed` | The lock is open, and the registry doesn't list the supplier | The supplier's check passes, or the business names the supplier |
| `supplier.over_open_cap` | The reserve would pass the most the lock holds at once with suppliers the business didn't name | Open commitments with those suppliers end, or the business raises the cap with its root key |

Naming or removing a supplier takes the business account's root key or one of its admin keys. Opening the lock or setting its cap takes the root key. No agent's key does any of them.

A lock from Moderato's first factory has no supplier registry, so it can't be opened and takes only the suppliers its business named.

## Start and read a check

```ts
import { rootRequestKey, sandboxBusinessChecks } from '@avow-finance/sdk'

const checks = sandboxBusinessChecks({
  baseUrl: 'https://api.testnet.avow.finance',
  key: rootRequestKey(root),
  domain: { chainId: 42431, verifyingContract: lock },
})

const check = await checks.start()
if (check.state === 'started') console.log(`Finish the check at ${check.url}`)

const now = await checks.read()
```

`root` is the business account's root key and `lock` is the business's own lock. Avow refuses any other signer with `request.signer_unknown`, so an agent's key can neither start a check of its business nor read one.

`start` is safe to call again. A check that's open, in review or passed comes back as it stands, and only a business with no check, or one whose check was declined or lapsed, starts a new one. `read` asks the checker again while the check is open, so polling it follows the checker's decision.

## States

| State | Means | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `not_available` | This deployment runs no checker, so `start` started nothing | Nothing |
| `not_started` | The business never started a check, or its last one lapsed unfinished | Nothing |
| `started` | The check waits for a person on the checker's page | `by`, `simulated`, `url` |
| `in_review` | The checker has what it asked for and hasn't decided | `by`, `simulated` |
| `passed` | The checker passed the business | `by`, `simulated`, `decidedAt` |
| `declined` | The checker declined the business | `by`, `simulated`, `decidedAt` |

`decidedAt` is when Avow recorded the decision, in unix seconds as a decimal string.

## Refusals

| Code | Status | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `request.signer_unknown` | 401 | The signer isn't a sandbox business's root key |
| `message.wrong_contract` | 400 | The request is signed under a lock that isn't the business's own |
| `request.invalid` | 400 | The request carries anything but an empty object |
| `request.in_progress` | 409 | Another request is asking the checker about this business, on `start` only |
| `service.unavailable` | 503 | Avow couldn't reach the checker to start the check |

The SDK sends a request again by itself after `request.in_progress` and after a 503, and Avow answers a repeat of a request it recorded with its first answer, so a lost answer never starts a second check.
