A service is something your business delivers by a deadline at a fixed price per unit, such as an hour of audio transcribed or a report written. The `service/1` template in `@avow-finance/spec` describes one, and a booking names the exact version of your offer it books. `serviceTermsMatchOffer` checks that terms carry that version's price and rules unchanged, so run it before you accept.

Version 1 covers one-off services at a fixed price. Subscriptions, usage billing and physical shipping aren't services under it, so don't list them as one.

## An offer and its versions

Your offer is a `ServiceOfferVersion`: what you sell, the price of one unit, the currency, and your rules for booking it. A version never changes. A new price or any change to the rules is a new version, and `offerVersionId` gives each one its own id, the hash of its content.

| Rule | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| `evidence` | What counts as evidence that you delivered |
| `leadTime` | How long after booking you deliver by, in seconds, at least 15 minutes so you have time to accept |
| `maxPerBooking` | The most units one booking takes |
| `dailyCapacity` | How many bookings you take in a day. The terms don't carry it, so whoever accepts bookings for you counts against it |
| `freeCancelWindow` | How long after booking the buyer can cancel at no charge. Zero turns it off, and otherwise it runs from 15 minutes to the lead time |
| `lateCancellationBps` | What a late cancellation can cost, as a share of the price |
| `noShowBps` | What the buyer not being there to take delivery can cost, as a share of the price |
| `payment` | How the buyer pays, `transfer` or `invoice`, and how long after delivery payment is due |

Every time counts from the moment the terms are built. `serviceOfferProblem` says what's wrong with a version in words you can show the person setting it, and `offerVersionId` won't give an id to a version it refuses. At least one charge has to be above zero, since a reserve with nothing to charge backs nothing. `parseServiceOfferVersion` reads a version from JSON and refuses any field it doesn't know, so two parties that read the same JSON get the same id.

## The terms a booking signs

`serviceOfferTerms(version, quantity, now, settlement)` builds the terms for booking a number of units at a moment. The buyer's agent and your side call it with the same version and get the same terms.

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `offer` | The id of the offer version booked |
| `item` | The item's name and one sentence on what it is |
| `quantity` | How many units are booked |
| `delivery` | The delivery deadline and what counts as evidence |
| `payment` | The payment route and when payment is due |

The price per unit is the consideration, a rate per `unit`, and `servicePrice` multiplies it by the quantity. The lock never reads it. The buyer pays it by the route the terms name, and the reserve doesn't secure it.

The reserve backs what the buyer owes if it walks away. A late cancellation is chargeable once free cancellation ends, and a no-show from the delivery deadline. Each costs its share of the whole price, and the reserve is the larger of the two. You accept within 15 minutes of the booking, payment is due at or after delivery, and you can report a charge until a day after the delivery deadline, when the reserve comes back.

Every service booking falls under the mandate category `service`, which `SERVICE_CATEGORY` names, so a buyer's mandate allows services by naming it. Service terms under any other category are refused as `terms.category_mismatch`.

## An order between two agents

A service order carries one booking of your offer between a buyer's agent and yours, wherever each runs. `ServiceOrderRequest` in `@avow-finance/spec` is what the buyer's agent sends: the whole offer version it wants, which names your business, a quantity, and the moment the terms are built from. Your agent answers with a `ServiceQuote`, which holds the terms, their salt and your signing key's agreement to them.

Both sides build the terms for themselves with `serviceOfferTerms`, from the order's offer, quantity and moment. The offer in an order is the buyer's copy, and a buyer can write any offer it likes under your name. So your agent runs `publishedOffer` on every order and quotes only a version you published. Nothing is agreed until you quote.

| Check | Who runs it | What it refuses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `serviceOrderProblem(order, now)` | Whoever takes the order | An offer the template refuses, a quantity the offer doesn't take, an order that has lapsed, and one that lapses less than five minutes before you can no longer accept |
| `publishedOffer(order, published)` | Your agent, before it quotes | An offer that is none of the versions you published |
| `serviceQuoteProblem(order, quote, settlement)` | The buyer's agent, before it reserves | Terms that differ from the order's in any way, and an agreement that isn't for a service, for the order's commitment, for your business, or for the hash of those terms |

The buyer's agent checks the agreement's expiry and its own authority when it commits, and Avow checks your signature when it approves the reserve.

You accept within 15 minutes of the moment the terms are built from, so an order lapses five minutes before then. A version you've since replaced isn't quoted. Your agent declines the order instead, as [Declines and withdrawals](#declines-and-withdrawals) sets out, so the buyer's agent learns why.

A service order has paths of its own under `SANDBOX_PATHS`, apart from a job order's, so a reader that knows only jobs never meets one. Avow's testnet sandbox serves those paths. Against a sandbox that doesn't, `order`, `orders` and `sell` answer that it doesn't carry service orders, and `listen` says so once and listens for jobs only.

## Sell and buy with the sandbox package

The `avow-sandbox` package runs the whole order for both sides, from a terminal, from the MCP server's tools, or from code. The seller's agent is connected with `--sell`, and the buyer's mandate names the seller's business and the `service` category.

```bash
# The seller's folder. Publishing writes transcript.offer.json, which you give the buyer.
npx avow-sandbox offer publish --name "Transcript" \
  --summary "One hour of audio transcribed into English text" --price 50 \
  --evidence "The transcript is delivered as a text file" \
  --late-cancellation 20 --no-show 50 --max 10
npx avow-sandbox listen

# The buyer's folder.
npx avow-sandbox order --offer transcript.offer.json --quantity 2
```

| Step | Terminal | MCP tool | SDK |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publish an offer | `offer publish` | `publish_offer` | `publish(draft)` |
| See what's published | `offer list` | `offers` | `published()` |
| Find Avow's test sellers and their offers | | `test_sellers` | `sandboxTestSellers()` |
| Check an order before sending it | | `check` | `checkOrder({ offer, quantity })` on the agent |
| Order from an offer and see it through | `order --offer` | `order` | `buy({ offer, quantity })` |
| See what waits on you | | `orders` | `inbox()` and `unfinished()` |
| Quote, then accept | `listen` | `sell` | `sell(order)` or `listen()` |
| Decline an order to you | `decline <order> --reason <code>` | `decline` | `decline(orderId, decline)` |
| Withdraw an order you sent | `withdraw <order>` | `withdraw_order` | `withdrawOrder(orderId)` |
| Read a thread order | `thread <order>` | `thread` | `readThread(orderId)` |

`sandboxServices()` in `@avow-finance/sdk` gives the calls in the last column, and `sandboxAgent()` gives `checkOrder`.

A check sends no order. It answers whether the order would pass the buyer's mandate now, with the price and the reserve, or the reason code and the layer that would refuse it. The reserve is what the mandate's limits count. No limit counts the price, which is paid outside the lock.

Publishing keeps the offer in the seller's `.avow` folder and sends it nowhere, so the seller hands the offer to the buyer, as the file `offer publish` writes or the JSON `publish_offer` answers with. Publishing an item of the same name again replaces its offer. [Listing offers with Avow](/docs/reference/offer-listings) sends a published version to Avow's directory, checked against the seller's mandate.

The buyer's side is checked three ways, by the mandate check, Avow's approval and the keychain. The seller's side has the agent's signing key and the rules below, which its own agent keeps. When Avow serves a [seller's mandate](/docs/reference/seller-mandate) for the agent's key, the agent also holds itself to it before it publishes, quotes or accepts. Avow serves none yet, so today an agent sells as it did before and says that no seller's mandate is in force and that Avow doesn't enforce seller limits yet. So the price and both charges are the person's to set, and an agent that publishes an offer asks for them.

- It quotes only an offer found among the ones it published, with `publishedOffer`. An order for a version you replaced is declined as `offer.superseded`, with the version you publish now. An order for an item you don't publish is declined as `offer.withdrawn`.
- It counts against the offer's `dailyCapacity` as it quotes, and quotes nothing past it. An order past it is declined as `capacity.exhausted`, with when the soonest place frees. A quote holds its place until you can no longer accept the order, 15 minutes after its terms were built, and an order you accepted holds its place for 24 hours. A declined order holds none. The count is by item name and lives in the folder, so run one seller on a folder at a time.
- It quotes the terms the order builds and nothing else, so there is no price to negotiate in an order.
- It accepts only an order whose quote it signed.

The buyer's agent keeps each order in `.avow` before it sends it, and checks the quote against that copy, whatever the mailbox says the order was. Ordering the same offer and quantity again picks up an order that's still open, and sends a new one once the first has ended. It funds nothing the lock already holds. A funded order is listed with the agent's other commitments, with a link anyone can check it by.

A booking's reserve backs the two charges the offer names and nothing else. The price is paid by the offer's payment route, and nothing in the order pays it or shows that it was paid.

## Declines and withdrawals

An order can end before anything is reserved in three ways. Nobody answers and it lapses, the seller's agent declines it with a reason, or the buyer's agent withdraws it. A decline and a withdrawal reach the other side at once, so neither waits for a lapse to learn what happened. Both are mailbox steps for jobs and services alike, on each kind's own paths, and Avow answers either the same when it's sent again.

A decline carries a reason code from a fixed list and only the fields that reason allows, and no free text. `orderDeclineProblem` in `@avow-finance/spec` checks one, and `DECLINE_RULES` gives each reason the next step the buyer's agent takes.

| Reason | Who sends it | Next step | It can also carry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `offer.superseded` | The seller, for a service | `change_order` | The version that replaced the one ordered, whole, which it has to carry |
| `offer.withdrawn` | The seller, for a service | `choose_another` | |
| `capacity.exhausted` | The seller | `retry_later` | When to ask again, and for a service how many units are free |
| `quantity.out_of_range` | The seller, for a service | `change_order` | How many units the seller would take |
| `proposal.unacceptable` | The seller | `change_order` | |
| `counterparty.not_eligible` | The seller | `choose_another` | |
| `supplier.not_authorized` | The seller | `choose_another` | |

- **A seller's own limits stay its own.** When the seller's mandate refuses an order, the agent declines with `supplier.not_authorized`. It does the same where Avow's seller's mandate checks are on and Avow refuses its quote. Those checks are off on the sandbox until a deploy turns them on. The step it reports keeps its own reason, which never leaves its machine. `supplierDeclineReason` turns any refusal into the reason it may send.
- **What a buyer may hear.** A seller sends a reason the seller's mandate check also gives only once `BUYER_HEARS` lists it. So `approval.human_required` isn't sent today, since Avow takes no request to approve one sale and a buyer told to wait would wait for nothing.
- **When to ask again is advice.** It falls after the order was taken and within seven days, and nothing holds either side to it.
- **A replaced offer is a new offer.** The buyer's agent checks the replacing version as it checks any offer it has never seen, its mandate included, before it orders it. No step says to send the same order again unchanged.
- **The buyer withdraws its own order.** Any active agent of the buying business can withdraw it, so an agent revoked since it ordered doesn't leave the order with nobody to end it. Any quote that came back stays on the record and can no longer be funded.
- **Expiry.** A quoted order past its expiry can still be declined or withdrawn, while the lock holds nothing for it. An order that lapsed unquoted stays lapsed.
- **Freed capacity.** A decline or a withdrawal after a quote frees the seller's capacity at once, in the agent's folder, and in Avow's count where its seller's mandate checks are on.
- **No reserve for an ended order.** Avow approves no reserve for an order that was declined or withdrawn, and refuses it with `order.declined` or `order.withdrawn`.
- **Once funded, the lock decides.** Neither step is taken once the buyer's lock holds the reserve, and the lock's own deadlines govern from then on. A side that ends an order at the moment the buyer funds it can lose that race, and the reserve then goes back at the acceptance deadline.

`buy` ends on a decline or a withdrawal with the reason, and `order`'s answer and `orders` name the reason and the next step.

A thread order carries a service order as a [negotiation thread](/docs/reference/negotiation), on paths of its own. There a decline is a signed move by the side that declines, and a withdrawal is the buyer's decline with `order.withdrawn`. `readThread` and the `thread` tool replay a thread with `threadProblem` before they say where it stands.

## Order from a test seller

Avow's sandbox runs test sellers, labelled test businesses that quote and accept simple service orders at any hour, so a newly connected agent has something real to order before any business hands it an offer. Each one's name ends "(test business)", each item it sells starts "Test", and nothing it sells is delivered or billed. A test business is never verified, so call each one a test business wherever you show it.

The sandbox lists them in the deployment file it publishes, the same `config.json` the SDK reads Avow's API and factory from, under `testSellers`. Each entry has the seller's business ID, its name and its offers, whole. `sandboxTestSellers()` reads the list, so it's as trustworthy as the sandbox's own origin, and the mandate your person approved is what stops an order. It answers `{ listed: false }` when the sandbox lists none. It refuses the whole list when any seller has a name without the label, an item that doesn't start "Test", text outside one-line printable ASCII, or one of the words `TEST_SELLER_REFUSED_WORDS` lists, such as verified, certified, KYB or vetted, in any of its words. The check reads five stand-ins as letters, 0 as o, 1, l and | as i, and 3 as e, and nothing else. Avow writes the list itself, so the word check is a backstop, and it refuses those words and nothing more.

```ts
import { sandboxServices, sandboxTestSellers } from '@avow-finance/sdk'

const found = await sandboxTestSellers()
if (!found.listed) throw new Error('The sandbox lists no test sellers')
const [seller] = found.sellers
const ping = seller.offers.find((o) => o.item.name === 'Test ping')
const outcome = await (await sandboxServices()).buy({ offer: ping, quantity: 1 })
```

From a model, call `test_sellers`, then `check` and `order` with one of the offers it lists, as it lists it. The buyer's mandate has to name the seller's business, as for any supplier. When `check` says the supplier isn't allowed, ask with `request_change` to name it, and the administrator decides.

## When the offer changes

A booking signed under a version keeps that version's terms, whatever you change later. Terms never change after the agreement. The reason codes `offer.superseded` and `offer.withdrawn` are for refusing a booking of a version you've since replaced or withdrawn. A superseded refusal should carry the current version, so the agent can show the new price before it books again.

Publishing a new price, or withdrawing the offer, changes nothing about an order you've already quoted. Three things hold the terms where they were signed.

- The quote carries the terms whole, and your agreement signs their hash. The buyer's lock holds the commitment under that same hash, so terms that differ by a byte don't match it.
- The order carries the offer version whole. Anyone holding the order can build its terms again with `serviceOfferTerms`, hash them under the quote's salt with `termsHash`, and compare the result with the agreement and with the lock. That check needs nothing from your folder and nothing from Avow.
- The sandbox package's seller accepts by the hash of the agreement it signed. It never reads the offer again once it has quoted, so an edited offer can't reach an order already quoted.

Your edit takes effect on the next order. An order for the version you replaced or withdrew isn't quoted.

A service relies on no other booking in version 1, so its terms are always version 2.
