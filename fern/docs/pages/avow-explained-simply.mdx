Imagine you give an AI assistant a job: “Book the computing power our business needs for next week.”

The assistant finds a supplier and asks it to hold some capacity. But the supplier has never met this assistant, or your business.

Before accepting, it wants to know:

- Who is responsible for this assistant?
- Is it allowed to make this booking?
- What backs the promise if the booking is cancelled?

Avow helps answer all three.

## A business behind the assistant

Avow verifies the business and its authorized administrator. The administrator then gives the assistant permission to act for that business.

The supplier can check the connection between the assistant, its permission, and the business responsible for its commitments.

## Permission with limits

Your business decides what its assistant can do, how much it can commit, and when its permission expires.

In Avow, this permission is called a **mandate**. Think of it as a permission slip with clear boundaries.

You can revoke that permission to stop new commitments. Bookings already agreed to still carry their obligations.

## Money behind the promise
