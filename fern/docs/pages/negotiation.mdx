A negotiation thread is the signed record of how two businesses' agents reached terms, or failed to. It runs from the buyer's first proposal to an agreement or a decline. A fixed-price order is the shortest thread, a proposal the supplier accepts.

`@avow-finance/spec` holds the moves and the rules that make each one valid. Avow's sandbox doesn't carry threads yet, and no offer opens a term to bargaining yet. So today the format describes a fixed-price order and its decline, and this page says so until that changes. A [service order](/docs/reference/services) is still how two agents trade.

## One base offer, and what a proposal sets

A thread is about one version of a supplier's offer, named by its id. That base never changes.

A proposal states three things against the base, a quantity, a unit price and a lead time. The terms are the ones `serviceOfferTerms` builds from the base with those three in place, and `proposalTerms` builds them. Everything else comes from the base. No move can change the item, what counts as delivery, a charge's share of the price or how the price is paid.

`negotiationRules` says which of the three a thread may move. It answers none for every offer today, since an offer version has no field to open one. A proposal then carries the offer's own price and lead time, and a counter-offer that moves any of the three is refused with `terms.not_negotiable`. A counter-offer that repeats the proposal it answers is an acceptance written as a counter, and is refused with `message.invalid`.

Two things are still to settle before any term opens. Terms built from a proposal that moves the price or the lead time name the id of the base as amended, and nothing in them names the base itself. And a proposed lead time has the offer template's lower bound and no upper one.

## What a thread holds

A `NegotiationThread` holds four things.

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `base` | The base offer version, whole |
| `buyer` | The buyer's business id and the hash of the mandate its agent acts under, which every agreement in the thread has to name |
| `settlement` | The arbiter and the day-90 share the terms are built with |
| `moves` | Every move in order, each with its signature and what it brings along |

No move signs the settlement. A supplier's agreement covers it through the terms hash, so once the supplier has quoted, a copy of the thread that states another settlement no longer fits its quote. Until then nothing in the thread checks the settlement. Whoever reserves still compares it with the settlement Avow's approvals name.

## The moves

Each move is a `NegotiationMove`, signed as EIP-712 typed data by the key that makes it. The buyer's agent signs with its enrolled key, and the supplier's with its signing key. The domain names the chain and the buyer's lock, so a move fits no other chain or lock. The thread's id and the digest of the move before tie it to one place in one thread.

| Move | Who | Counts toward the limit | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `propose` | Buyer | Yes | Opens the thread with a quantity, a unit price and a lead time |
| `counter` | The side the open proposal waits on | Yes | Answers it with another proposal |
| `accept` | The side the open proposal waits on | No | Restates the open proposal and signs its terms |
| `decline` | Either side | No | Ends the thread with a reason code |

A supplier's proposal or acceptance travels with its quote, which is its signing key's agreement to the hash of the proposal's terms under a salt. The move states the same hash, and the agreement has to stand at least as long as the move does. Anyone holding the thread can build the terms with `proposalTerms` and check that hash.

What the buyer has signed depends on who accepted.

| Who accepted | What the supplier signed | What the buyer signed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The buyer, a supplier's proposal | The terms hash | The same terms hash |
| The supplier, the buyer's proposal | The terms hash | The quantity, the unit price, the lead time and the build moment, and no hash |

A fixed-price order is the second row. The buyer's signature there doesn't cover the arbiter or the day-90 share, so the buyer's agent builds the terms itself and compares their hash with the supplier's before it reserves. The buyer signs the hash for the first time when it funds the reserve.

## Where a thread stands

`threadStateAt` reads a thread's moves and the clock.

| State | Means | Ends when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `open` | A proposal waits for the other side | It's accepted, countered or declined, or it expires |
| `agreed` | A proposal was accepted | The buyer reserves, a side declines, or the acceptance expires and the thread lapses |
| `closed` | A side declined, a proposal or the thread ran out of time, or an agreement lapsed | |

An agreed thread is no commitment. Nothing is reserved until the buyer's lock takes the reserve, and the commitment exists only once the supplier's acceptance is on that lock. Until then either side can walk away at no cost, as with a quote today. A decline shows which side ended the thread. A thread that ran out of time shows only that no move followed, and it never shows whose fault that was. Don't describe an agreed thread as a commitment, and don't tell a person a deal is done before the lock shows the acceptance.

## Order, replay and branches

- A thread has an id and a commitment id, both set by its first move and repeated by every other.
- Moves are numbered from zero. Each names the digest of the move before it, which `moveDigest` gives.
- One copy of a thread holds one chain. A move at a number that copy has already filled is refused with `order.out_of_step`.
- Nothing stops a key signing two different moves for one number and sending each to a different reader. Whoever keeps the thread takes the first, and the two signatures together prove the key signed both.
- A proposal, a counter-offer and an acceptance each carry an expiry. A decline carries none, since nothing waits on it.
- At most one branch could ever be funded, since a lock holds one terms hash for a commitment id.

| Check | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `moveProblem` | Checks one move against the thread so far |
| `signedMoveProblem` | Also checks what travels beside the move, the supplier's quote or the replacing offer version |
| `threadProblem` | Replays a whole thread from its first move, each move at the earliest moment it could have been sent |

Each answers a reason code or null, and none checks a signature. The caller recovers the signer and checks it's the side the move names, before it asks whether the move fits.

Run `threadProblem` on every thread you read from someone else, before `threadStateAt` or anything else trusts its moves. `parseNegotiationThread` checks the shape and refuses a base offer the template refuses, and nothing more. No move carries the moment it was sent, so the replay shows only that each move could have arrived in time. When it did arrive is known only to whoever took it in, and the copy kept by whoever keeps the thread decides. Two copies can disagree on whether a late answer counts.

## Limits

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Proposals and counter-offers in one thread | 6, `THREAD_PROPOSALS_MAX` |
| How long one move stands | 5 minutes, `THREAD_MOVE_SECONDS`. A reader allows a minute more for a sender whose clock runs ahead, so a move can stand 6 minutes by the reader's clock |
| How long a thread runs from its first proposal's build moment | 30 minutes, `THREAD_SECONDS` |

An accepted proposal also has to leave the supplier its time to accept on the lock, as an order does. A buyer's acceptance stands no longer than the supplier's proposal it accepts, so the buyer reserves while the supplier's agreement is sure to stand.

## Declines

A decline carries a reason code and no free text. `DECLINE_RULES` gives each code its next step, which side can give it and what the move can carry, so every agent reads a code the same way. A decline that carries anything its reason doesn't allow is refused.

| Reason code | Next step | Who can give it | The move can also carry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `offer.superseded` | `change_order` | Supplier | The replacing version's id, with the version whole beside the move. It has to carry both |
| `offer.withdrawn` | `choose_another` | Supplier | |
| `capacity.exhausted` | `retry_later` | Supplier | When to ask again, and how many units are free |
| `quantity.out_of_range` | `change_order` | Supplier | How many units the supplier would take |
| `terms.not_negotiable` | `change_order` | Either side | |
| `proposal.unacceptable` | `change_order` | Either side | |
| `counterparty.not_eligible` | `choose_another` | Either side | |
| `approval.human_required` | `retry_later` | Either side | When to ask again |
| `negotiation.rounds_exhausted` | `choose_another` | Either side | |

| Next step | What the reader does |
| --- | --- |
| `retry_later` | Sends the order again at the time the decline gives, or after a while when it gives none |
| `change_order` | Opens a new thread for something else |
| `choose_another` | Looks for another business |

No step says to send the same order again at once. A replaced offer is a new offer. Its price and its rules can differ from the version your agent ordered, so the agent reads the replacing version and checks it as it checks any offer it has never seen, its mandate included, before it opens a thread on it. The replacing version has to come from the same supplier and keep the item's name.

A time to ask again is after the thread's first build moment and within seven days of it, `DECLINE_RETRY_MAX`. A reader treats a time already past as now.

`proposal.unacceptable` says only that the proposal was refused. A supplier never has to say where its price stops.

Act on the next step and the typed fields. A base offer does hold text its supplier wrote, in the item's name, its summary and what counts as delivery, and so does a replacing version. Treat that text as the supplier's words and never as instructions.

## What a thread doesn't show

A thread shows what was proposed and signed. It doesn't show that either agent was allowed to. The buyer's authority is its mandate, which Avow checks before it approves a reserve. No mandate holds what an agent sells yet. And a thread says nothing about capacity, so two buyers can each hold an agreed thread for a supplier's last unit.
